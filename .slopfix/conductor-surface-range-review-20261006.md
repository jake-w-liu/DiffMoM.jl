# Conductor surface range review

Finite layered-film and two-sheet impedances previously became NaNs or lost a smaller representable component after gamma-square, thickness-square or product range loss. Independent Julia slab/transfer equations and 700/1400-digit Python calculations confirm the failures. The public entry point is now checked separately from the numerical helper: an earlier prototype passed its isolated helper but returned 14 finite values with 50% reactance error on a wider grid. All failures and invalid harness expectations are retained in ignored local audit evidence.

Recovery is entered before subnormal or lost product components reach the small-q transfer quotient. Fully widened material/frequency inputs bypass recursive recovery; mixed inputs retain it. General loaded, plated and complex cascades use the supported scoped BigFloat precision do-block, preserving higher caller and owned input precision. Growing exponentials are factored consistently from all transfer entries. A positive Float64 single open film uses exponent-balanced products and separate real/imaginary evaluation in the recovery path. Ordinary arithmetic remains unchanged. The two-sheet wrapper also detects a lost subnormal component during thickness halving and performs the physical halving after widening, before casting the finite impedance.

Both Julia 1.12.7 and 1.13.1 pass 958 focused assertions: 52 original and 906 new regression checks. The public grid has 254 cases, including 244 representable values and 10 explicit output-range rejections, with unchanged component gates. Independent full transfer matrices qualify 32 loaded/plated/complex/bare cases. Sixty-six ordinary controls per version retain response bits and five-sample allocation minima. Four concurrent two-layer wide recoveries preserve precision scopes. Four additional independently checked real/complex subnormal half-thickness cases recover on both versions. Three rare public Float64 single-film cases now each have five zero-byte warmed Julia allocation samples, replacing approximately 503 KB per call in the earlier wide prototype. Single-call timings do not support a speedup ratio; opaque MPFR allocations, peak RSS, unrestricted numerical range and native Volume Loss remain unqualified. Full cumulative package and committed-source quality/hosted CI checks are separate publication gates.
